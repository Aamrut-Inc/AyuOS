# What AyuOS is

AyuOS is a personal health operating system. It brings all of a person's
health information — the data from their wearable devices, their hospital
and medical records, and anything they track themselves — into one place
that they actually own and control, and then helps them make sense of it.

## The problem it's solving

Right now, a person's health picture is scattered across places that don't
talk to each other. A smartwatch app knows their heart rate and sleep. A
hospital portal knows their diagnoses, lab results, and medications. Neither
one has any idea the other exists, and neither gives a person a real,
combined understanding of their own health. If something's genuinely worth
paying attention to — a pattern that only makes sense when you look at the
wearable trends *and* the medical history together — there's currently no
single place that could even see it, let alone explain it.

AyuOS exists to fix that: pull everything into one unified, coherent picture,
under the person's own control, instead of leaving it fragmented across five
different apps and portals that were never designed to talk to each other.

## Where it runs, and why that matters

AyuOS runs entirely on a person's own device. Their health data doesn't get
uploaded to some company's servers to be pooled, sold, or analyzed by anyone
else — it stays local, under their own control, the same way a diary would.
That's not an implementation detail, it's a deliberate stance: this is some
of the most sensitive information a person has, and AyuOS is built on the
premise that they shouldn't have to hand it over to a third party just to
get value from it.

## The AI layer being built on top

Once someone's data is unified in one place, AyuOS is being built to reason
over it — to actually answer the kinds of questions a person has about their
own health, using their real data and real, cited medical research, not
generic advice a chatbot would give anyone. If it doesn't have enough
information to answer well, it's designed to say so and ask — either by
looking for more of the person's own data, or by asking them directly —
rather than guessing.

## Who it's for

Anyone who wants an honest, complete picture of their own health instead of
a fragmented one, and who wants answers grounded in their actual data and
real research when they ask a question about it — not a black box, and not
somewhere else's server holding their most personal information.
